The International Space Station (ISS) is a modular space station in low Earth orbit. It is a joint project among five participating space agencies: NASA (United States), Roscosmos (Russia), JAXA (Japan), ESA (European Space Agency), and CSA (Canada). The ownership and use of the space station is established by intergovernmental treaties and agreements. The ISS is suited for the testing of spacecraft systems and equipment required for missions to the Moon and Mars, and also provides a microgravity and space environment in which scientific research is conducted in astrobiology, astronomy, meteorology, physics, and other fields.

The station has been continuously inhabited since the arrival of Expedition 1 in November 2000, making it the longest continuous human presence in space. As of 2021, more than 240 people from 19 different countries have visited the ISS. The ISS has been serviced by a variety of launch vehicles, spacecraft, and crew vehicles, including the Russian Soyuz and Progress spacecraft, the U.S. Space Shuttle (until its retirement in 2011), SpaceX's Crew Dragon, Russia’s Soyuz-MS series, and others.

Structurally, the ISS consists of multiple interconnected modules, trusses, solar arrays, radiators, docking ports, and other systems. The modules include the U.S. Destiny Laboratory Module, the European Columbus laboratory, the Japanese Kibo module (which includes an external experiment platform), and Russian modules such as Zvezda and Zarya. The station's truss segments support large solar arrays which convert sunlight into electricity; the arrays can produce up to 120 kilowatts of power under optimal conditions. The electrical power system includes batteries and power distribution units to ensure continuous power during orbital night periods.

The attitude control and thermal control systems maintain the station in a stable orientation relative to Earth and manage the internal and external temperatures of the structure. The station uses gyroscopes, reaction control thrusters, and control moment gyroscopes to maintain attitude. Radiators on the truss emit waste heat into space, and thermal blankets and insulation protect equipment and modules from extreme temperature swings.

Life support systems aboard the ISS supply air, water, and temperature regulation.
